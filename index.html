Buying Property Without a Realtor

A comprehensive guide for unrepresented buyers in British Columbia. Save thousands in commission while staying protected.

Potential savings: Buyer agent commissions in BC typically range from 2.5% to 3.5% of the purchase price. On a $900,000 property, that is $22,500 to $31,500.

Direct communication: Negotiate directly with the seller's agent, reducing miscommunication and delays.

Full control: You decide the offer strategy, timeline, and conditions without pressure from a third party.

More leverage: Some sellers prefer unrepresented buyers because it reduces their total commission costs, which can make your offer more attractive.

Important: Always engage a real estate lawyer or notary to review your Contract of Purchase and Sale before signing. This is non-negotiable regardless of whether you have a realtor.

  1. Research & shortlist — Use this dashboard to find and compare properties. Analyze deal scores, neighborhood trends, and price benchmarks.
  2. View properties — Contact the listing agent directly to arrange viewings. You have the right to view any listed property.
  3. Prepare your offer — Use the Offer Generator tab to create a professional offer letter with appropriate pricing based on our deal analysis.
  4. Submit the offer — Send your offer to the listing agent via email (use our Email to Agent feature). Include subject clauses to protect yourself.
  5. Negotiate — The seller may accept, reject, or counter. Be prepared to negotiate on price, dates, and conditions.
  6. Subject removal — Complete your due diligence (inspection, financing, title search) within the subject period (typically 7-14 days).
  7. Deposit — Deliver your deposit (typically 5% of purchase price) to the trust account specified in the contract.
  8. Completion & possession — Your lawyer/notary handles the title transfer, mortgage registration, and key exchange on completion day.

Waiving subjects: Never remove subject clauses (especially inspection and financing) to "win" a bidding war. The risk far outweighs the reward.
Skipping the title search: Hidden liens, easements, or rights-of-way can dramatically affect property value and your rights.
Not budgeting for closing costs: Property Transfer Tax, legal fees, inspection, appraisal, and insurance can add 1.5%-3% to your purchase price.
Ignoring strata documents: For condos/townhouses, review the last 2 years of strata minutes for upcoming special levies, litigation, or major repairs.
Emotional bidding: Set a maximum price before you offer and stick to it. Use our Deal Score to stay objective.

Unsolicited Property Acquisition

Approach property owners directly — even when their home isn't listed for sale. Many homeowners will consider selling if presented with a serious, well-structured offer. This tool helps you research target properties, draft professional letters of intent, and manage your outreach campaigns.

IMPORTANT: In BC, the Home Buyer Rescission Period (3 business days, 0.25% fee) applies to private residential sales just as it does to MLS transactions. You still need a BC lawyer or notary to handle conveyancing. The standard Contract of Purchase and Sale (BCREA form) is recommended even for private transactions.

Private Sale Guide

Legal basis: Anyone can make an offer on any property in BC, listed or not.

Standard process: Letter of intent → Discussion → Contract of Purchase and Sale → Subject period → Completion

BC-specific: The Home Buyer Rescission Period applies to private sales (3 business days, 0.25% fee).

Commission savings: The seller saves 4-7% in agent commissions, which can be used as a negotiating lever. Offer to split the savings — e.g., "You save $40K in commissions, I'm asking for a $20K discount."

BC Land Title & Survey Authority (ltsa.ca): Title search gives owner name, legal description, PID, charges/liens. Cost: ~$15-30 per search via BC OnLine.

BC Assessment (bcassessment.ca): Free lookup for assessed value, lot size, year built, property class.

City/Municipal records: Zoning, permits, property tax amounts.

Physical approaches: Check mailbox name, knock on door, leave a letter.

Note: Do NOT use aggressive or harassing tactics. One polite approach is appropriate. Respect "no" answers.

Start with BC Assessment value — reflects market value as of July 1 of previous year.

Compare to recent sales in the neighborhood (available through LTSA or BC Assessment).

Consider: DOM of comparable listed properties, current market conditions, property condition.

Private sale discount: Sellers often expect 2-5% less than MLS price since they save on commission.

Rule of thumb: Offer 85-95% of estimated market value as starting point, depending on motivation.

Contract of Purchase and Sale: Use the BCREA standard form (available from your lawyer).

Property Transfer Tax: Same rates as MLS sales. FTHB exemptions apply.

Home Buyer Rescission Period: 3 business days, 0.25% fee — applies even without agents.

Title transfer: Must be done by a BC lawyer or notary.

Property Disclosure Statement: Not legally required in private sales (unlike MLS), but strongly recommended to request one.

GST: 5% GST may apply on new or substantially renovated homes — check with your lawyer.

Foreign buyer ban / speculation tax: Same rules apply as MLS sales.

"Hi, my name is [name]. I'm looking to buy a home in this neighborhood and I really like your property at [address]. I'm not an agent — I'm a private buyer. I understand you may not be thinking of selling, but if you'd ever consider it, I'd love to have a conversation. Here's my card/letter with my contact details. No pressure at all — just wanted to let you know there's genuine interest. Thank you for your time."

Keep it brief and friendly. Leave your letter of intent even if they seem uninterested — they may follow up later.

  • Seller refuses to provide title or property disclosure
  • Existing liens, judgments, or encumbrances on title
  • Property is in foreclosure or has conduct of sale orders
  • Seller wants to close without lawyers
  • Significant discrepancy between asking price and assessment
  • Unpermitted additions or renovations
  • Environmental contamination (especially former gas stations, dry cleaners, industrial sites)

To display real street-level photos of each property instead of map tiles, configure a Google Maps API key with the Street View Static API enabled.

How to get a free API key:

  1. Go to Google Cloud Console
  2. Create a new project (or select an existing one)
  3. Navigate to APIs & Services → Library
  4. Search for and enable Street View Static API
  5. Go to APIs & Services → Credentials
  6. Click Create Credentials → API Key
  7. Copy the key and paste it above

Google provides $200/month in free credits (~28,000 Street View requests). Your key is stored locally in your browser and never sent to any server except Google.

When the GitHub Feedback Integration is configured (below), offer generation events are automatically tracked as GitHub Issues with the offer-tracking label.

What gets tracked:

  • Buyer name, email, phone, and company
  • Property address, asking price, and offer price
  • Property type and timestamp

Each offer creates a GitHub Issue in your configured repository. Configure your GitHub token and repo in the GitHub Feedback Integration section below to activate.

Connect the Suggestions & Feedback form to GitHub Issues so that every submission creates a trackable issue in your repository with appropriate labels.

How to create a Personal Access Token:

  1. Go to GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens
  2. Click Generate new token (fine-grained recommended)
  3. Under Repository access, select your dashboard repository
  4. Under Permissions → Issues, grant Read and write access
  5. Generate the token and paste it above along with the repo name

Feedback submissions will create GitHub Issues with labels matching the feedback type (bug, enhancement, feedback). Your token and repo are stored locally in your browser and only sent to the GitHub API.

This dashboard is a free tool designed to help property buyers — especially those choosing not to use a realtor — find undervalued properties, analyze neighborhood trends, and generate professional offer letters.

Here is how to get started:

  1. Use the sidebar filters on the left to narrow down by region, price range, property type, and more.
  2. Browse properties in All Listings (table or card view) and click any property for details.
  3. Star properties you like to add them to your Shortlist.
  4. When ready, go to the Offer Generator to create a professional offer letter.
All Listings: Browse all properties with sorting and filtering. Toggle between table and card view. Click any property for detailed analysis.
Map View: See properties plotted on an interactive map. Clustered markers expand as you zoom in. Click a marker to see property details.
Neighborhood Analysis: Compare neighborhoods side-by-side with price data, market trends, year-over-year changes, and inventory levels. Click a neighborhood row to filter to that area.
Deal Analysis: See the top 10 best deals, score distribution, property type breakdown, and price distribution charts.
Offer Generator: Select a property, enter your buyer details, customize offer terms, and generate a professional PDF offer letter. Download it or email it directly to the listing agent.
Shortlist: Your saved properties. Export to CSV for your records. See total investment summary and aggressive offer totals.
